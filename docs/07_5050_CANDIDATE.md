[GIGABYTE official specifications](https://www.gigabyte.com/Graphics-Card/GV-N5050WF2OC-8GD/sp): 8 GB GDDR6; 199 x 116 x 40 mm; one 8-pin power connector. Use these dimensions in integrated four-card packing studies; neither a box calculation nor a nominal bay allowance certifies receiver/support/cable fit. Its published 550 W system recommendation is not a four-card pod power requirement.

Four cards contribute 32 GB nominal aggregate VRAM. With the separate 16 GB 5070 Ti, nominal inventory would be 48 GB across five cards **for host-staged placement only**. **Motherboard x4/x4/x4/x4 bifurcation does NOT create a P2P domain** — the four E1 branches remain separate CPU root ports, which is precisely the PCI_P2PDMA_MAP_NOT_SUPPORTED case, and each of those root ports must independently pass the peer gate. That gate is the **kernel's chipset allow-list plus BIOS ACS/IOMMU state, not the presence of a PCIe switch**: this host is AMD AM5 (`family >= 0x17`), where the allow-list passes with no switch, so a switch chassis is *not* a prerequisite for an Intel Arc arm here (a switch is only needed on a root complex that is not allow-listed, notably consumer Intel desktop chipsets). The x4 layout also halves peer bandwidth against Intel's own ~20 GB/s unidirectional / 40 GB/s bidirectional Gen5 x8 calibration, so fewer cards on wider links out-peer four cards at x4. "All four E1 branches used" is a bandwidth and mechanical statement, not a peer-capability statement; qualify the separate display route. The 5070 Ti can participate in a **mixed-vendor host-staged** matrix (20 directed pairs) but can never be a **Level Zero** peer — the four-card Level Zero directed matrix stays at 12. Usable capacity is lower after display/allocator/workspace/context/KV overhead. It is not one transparent 48 GB allocation.

The first experiment would be all-NVIDIA CUDA layer splitting, then a Vulkan comparison. PCIe Gen5 card capability does not change the proposed Gen4 x4 external link limit. Design against ~6.5 GB/s realistic one-way on a Gen4 x4 branch, **~3.2 GB/s for a host-staged round trip**, ~6.5 GB/s pipelined double-buffer; a 4 MiB boundary crossing at pipelined 6.5 GB/s is ~0.6 ms. Single-request decode traverses pipeline stages sequentially, so four cards do not give 4× speed — **expect capacity wins, not throughput wins**. Note that even all-NVIDIA consumer PCIe cards have no usable direct P2P: ThunderEP (arXiv:2609.40093) is a 2026 paper built entirely around optimizing CPU-memory-traversing transfers on RTX 4090/5090, which is strong evidence the host-staged path is the real baseline. Verify the exact OC board-power limit before selecting the PSU; do not freeze power sizing from the model family alone.

Keep the card choice open, subject to two hard constraints. (a) **For pooled capacity, four same-vendor cards strictly dominate any mixed-vendor population** — a mixed population can only ever use the host-staged path, while a same-vendor population also gets that vendor's own P2P subject to a directed probe. Four identical 5050s is therefore the dominant form of this candidate. (b) An **Intel** comparison is only meaningful for **Arc Pro B60 (24 GB) / B70 (32 GB)** (DELEGATED — not first-hand verified in-session), same generation and family, on **Linux** with PCIe ACS and IOMMU disabled. **No PCIe switch is required on this AMD AM5 host**: the gate is the kernel's chipset allow-list, which an AMD Zen host (`family >= 0x17`) passes without a switch (doc 10 §1.10, first-hand — 2× Arc Pro B60 peering at PCIe 5.0 x8 on an ordinary AM5 board, and *"Verified P2P works on AM5 host with 7.1.2 kernel, Intel Compute 26.22.38646.4"*). A switch is only needed on a root complex that is *not* allow-listed, notably a consumer Intel desktop; and every verified-working rig uses **x8** links, not x4. **Consumer Arc B580/B570/A770 carry no Intel multi-GPU peer qualification**; Intel's own table marks diff-gen dGPU+dGPU and iGPU+dGPU as No. Note also that Intel peer access is a **~15% throughput optimization, not a capacity unlock** — it does not raise the 8 GB-per-card ceiling this candidate is being evaluated against. Use cost per usable resident model capacity and transfer-inclusive workload throughput, not just generation, aggregate GB or gaming frame rates.
